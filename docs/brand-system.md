# Bram: Workout Notes — Brand Kit v2.0

## 0) Brand lock

**Working name:** Bram  
**Descriptor:** Workout Notes  
**Primary fonts:** Adobe Caslon Pro + Suisse Int’l  
**Primary mascot:** black bear companion  
**Core palette:** charcoal, warm cream, signature violet, espresso-black bear tones

Bram is a notes-first strength-training app built around a calm black bear companion. The brand should feel more grounded and workout-legible than the earlier polar bear direction, while preserving the same Apple-esque simplicity, editorial restraint, and approachable UI philosophy.

The product should feel like:

> **A quiet black bear companion who remembers your training and helps you get stronger by writing naturally.**

This is the center of the brand. Everything else should orbit it.

---

## 1) Brand direction

## Core idea
Bram is a workout notes app. The user writes naturally about what they trained, how they feel, what equipment they have, or what they want to do. Bram quietly interprets the note, structures the workout, remembers preferences, tracks progressive overload, and suggests the next useful step.

### Brand thesis
**Strength tracking should feel as natural as writing a note.**

Bram removes the usual workout-app friction: manually building routines, tapping through endless sets, remembering past numbers, decoding charts, and translating gym intuition into structured progress.

The app should feel simple on the surface and quietly intelligent underneath.

### Emotional balance
- **Strong, not macho**
- **Friendly, not childish**
- **Premium, not sterile**
- **Simple, not plain**
- **Smart, not AI-branded**
- **Warm, not soft**
- **Companion-led, not pet-game**
- **Workout-focused, not gym-bro**

### Tone words
- Calm
- Strong
- Grounded
- Steady
- Helpful
- Observant
- Intentional
- Crafted
- Quietly intelligent
- Masculine in a restrained way

### The memorable contradiction
The brand should be remembered as:

> **a sturdy black bear companion inside a restrained premium workout notebook.**

That is the tension. Bram should be approachable enough to use daily, but serious enough that a lifter immediately understands this is a strength-training product, not a cute wellness journal.

## 2) Final creative direction

# Quiet Strength Bear

This direction combines:
- Apple-style product restraint
- notes-first simplicity
- Adobe Caslon Pro warmth and character
- Suisse Int’l precision and utility
- a black bear as the emotional and visual anchor
- violet as the instantly recognizable brand signal
- charcoal, cream, and espresso-black as the premium foundation

### Design metaphor
**A private workout notebook kept by a calm black bear who remembers your training history.**

Not:
- AI workout app

- macro tracker clone
- gamified fitness pet
- macho strength app
- Viking / outdoors / hunting brand

Yes:
- daily workout note
- black bear training companion

## 4) Logo and wordmark system

The pairing of **Adobe Caslon Pro Semibold** for “Bram” and **Suisse Int’l Medium** for “Workout Notes” or supporting text is the core brand lockup.

## Primary wordmark
**Bram**
- Font: Adobe Caslon Pro Semibold
- Case: Title Case
- Color: brand violet
- Tracking: tight to normal

Caslon gives Bram warmth, maturity, and memorability. It keeps the black bear from becoming too blunt or gym-mascot-like. The contrast is the point: a sturdy black bear with an editorial, refined wordmark.

- Case: Title Case or sentence case depending on context

## 5) Typography system

## Chosen fonts
- **Accent / brand serif:** Adobe Caslon Pro
- **Primary / product sans:** Suisse Int’l

This pairing is the foundation of the brand.

## Typography principle
# **Suisse runs the product. Caslon names the bear.**

Suisse Int’l handles the app interface. Adobe Caslon Pro appears when Bram needs to feel more human, branded, editorial, or emotionally memorable.

## Font roles

### Adobe Caslon Pro
Use for:
- Bram wordmark
- splash screen headline
- select onboarding lines
- badge names
- weekly recap titles
- empty-state statements
- gentle motivational phrases
- marketing hero copy
- rare “Bram says” moments

Avoid for:
- dense UI
- calendar grids
- settings lists
- chart labels
- workout tables
- exercise names in structured logs
- rep / set / weight data
- long note body text

### Suisse Int’l
Use for:
- all core app UI
- buttons
- inputs
- note text
- exercise data
- calendar labels
- settings
- navigation
- charts
- captions
- descriptors
- App Store screenshots
- marketing body copy

## Weight rules

### Adobe Caslon Pro
- **Semibold:** Bram wordmark, hero phrases, badge titles
- **Regular:** editorial callouts, softer recap language
- **Italic:** very rare; reflective microcopy only

### Suisse Int’l
- **Medium:** headings, buttons, active labels, nav, segmented controls
- **Regular / Book:** body text, note text, placeholders, secondary UI
- **Semibold / Bold:** use sparingly; avoid over-hardening the product

## Typography feel
- Do not make the UI overly typographic.
- Let Caslon create one memorable emotional moment per screen at most.
- Keep core product surfaces calm and sans-led.
- Use scale and spacing before extra weights.

## 7) Color philosophy

The app palette remains intentionally restrained:

> **charcoal black + warm cream + signature violet + espresso-black bear**

The polar bear direction used off-white fur as part of the core memory. Bram shifts that memory toward a more workout-legible black bear while keeping the same premium system.

### Key color jobs
- **Charcoal:** premium app foundation, quiet focus, dark-mode depth
- **Warm cream:** editorial warmth, light-mode paper feel, brand cards
- **Espresso-black fur:** strength, groundedness, mascot identity
- **Cream muzzle / ears:** warmth, facial readability, approachability
- **Violet:** Bram’s signature, active states, intelligence, notebook
- **Orange:** streaks, effort, completed work, PRs, momentum
- **Sage:** recovery, rest, readiness, balance
- **Blue:** bodyweight or secondary data only
- **Gold:** rare milestone or premium-feeling achievement

### Important shift
Violet is still the signature accent, but it now lives primarily through:
- the wordmark
- the notebook
- active states
- selected dates
- primary CTA moments
- intelligent note highlights

Do not rely on a scarf or accessory to carry the brand color. The violet notebook is the main mascot accent.

## 10) Illustration and mascot system

## Mascot role
Bram is the emotional anchor of the brand.

He should feel like the app is quietly maintained by a sturdy, observant black bear companion.

Bram is not decorative filler. Bram appears when his presence improves memory, warmth, or comprehension.

## Current mascot direction
The final mascot direction should preserve:
- espresso-black fur
- cream muzzle and inner ears
- sketchy dark linework
- violet workout notebook
- friendly, calm expression
- rounded but grounded body
- slight hand-drawn imperfection
- no scarf as the primary brand expression

## Mascot personality
- Calm
- Proud
- Helpful
- Patient
- Observant
- Slightly playful
- Grounded
- Never frantic
- Never sarcastic
- Never hyper-motivational

## Art rules
- Use limited colors: black-brown fur, cream muzzle/ears, dark line, violet notebook, subtle shadow
- Keep linework imperfect but controlled
- Keep Bram’s expression gentle and confident
- The notebook is the main brand-color carrier
- Avoid rendering the bear with too much dimensional realism
- Avoid making Bram look like a brown bear, grizzly, teddy bear, or mascot costume
- Avoid humanoid muscles, abs, tank tops, gym clothing, or bodybuilder anatomy
- Avoid making the eyes disappear into the fur; preserve tiny highlights and clean facial contrast

## Best Bram poses
- holding the violet workout notebook
- looking at a calendar
- sitting beside a barbell plate
- peeking from behind a note card
- pointing gently at a progress insight
- proud but understated after a workout
- resting with a towel on recovery day
- checking off a page in his notebook

## Avoid Bram as
- yelling coach
- bodybuilder bear
- shirtless humanized gym mascot
- mascot jumping everywhere
- trophy machine
- constant screen filler
- replacement for UI hierarchy

## Mascot presence rule
Bram can be more present in marketing, onboarding, badges, and empty states. In daily product usage, he should usually appear as a small icon, subtle illustration, or suggestion-card companion.

## 11) Mascot cleanup / production rules

The final mascot should feel hand-drawn but polished.

### Preserve
- same silhouette
- same expression
- same notebook shape
- same violet notebook color
- same espresso-black bear palette
- same hand-drawn line character

### Clean
- stray artifact specks along the full dark outline
- noisy brown/white pixels in dark linework
- notebook edge smudging
- white barbell icon smudging
- accidental glare on notebook edges

### Do not clean so much that
- the linework becomes sterile
- the bear feels vector-flat
- the hand-drawn character disappears
- the fur becomes pure black with no warmth
- the design changes unintentionally

## 12) App icon direction

The app icon needs to be simpler than the full bear illustration.

## Recommended direction
# Option A: Bram head + violet notebook cue

### Why
- most ownable
- instantly mascot-led
- reads better than a full-body bear at small size
- separates Bram from generic workout trackers
- preserves violet as the brand signal

### Icon structure
- background: warm cream or charcoal
- foreground: Bram’s black bear head
- detail: violet notebook corner, violet page tab, or subtle violet accent
- linework: simplified dark strokes
- expression: calm, friendly, confident

### Avoid
- full-body bear
- tiny full notebook details
- barbell icon as the main symbol
- too much line texture at small size
- text inside the app icon
- making the bear pure black with no readable facial contrast

## Secondary icon concept
A violet notebook with a subtle bear-ear silhouette or small barbell mark could work for alternate icons, but it should not be the primary brand icon.

- **Volume / progressive overload:** orange
- **Adherence / training rhythm:** violet
- **Recovery / fatigue balance:** sage
- **Bodyweight trend:** blue

### Chart principles
- one main color per chart
- neutral gridlines
- soft labels
- no rainbow data systems
- no aggressive finance-dashboard visuals
- no thick bars unless the visual story demands it
- written insight should carry more meaning than the graphic

### Chart style
- thin lines
- soft bars
- rounded endpoints
- muted axes
- low-contrast grid
- very minimal legends

### Chart copy style
Good:
- “A little more volume than last week.”
- “Your push days are trending stronger.”
- “Recovery looks uneven this week.”
- “You trained three times this week.”

Avoid:
- “AI optimized your hypertrophy protocol.”
- “Crush your next PR.”
- “Maximum gains detected.”
- “Beast mode activated.”

## 18) Motion and interaction rules

Motion should feel soft, responsive, and premium.

## Motion principles
- no cartoon bounce for core UI
- tiny playfulness allowed for Bram moments
- use fade, drift, and soft scale
- interpretation states should feel calm, not magical in a cheesy way

## Timing suggestions
- Tap / hover states: **120–180 ms**
- Card transitions: **220–320 ms**
- Sheet reveal: **300–420 ms**
- Mascot reward animation: **500–900 ms**, rare

## Bram-specific motion
Allowed:
- small blink
- tiny head tilt
- notebook lift
- subtle proud expression after streak

Avoid:
- constant animation
- bouncing mascot
- loud confetti every time
- mascot interrupting the workflow
- Clippy-style behavior

## 19) Voice and microcopy rules

Bram should sound like a calm training companion, not a hype coach.

## Voice qualities
- brief
- warm
- observant
- specific
- low-pressure
- never corny

- “Your legs may need a lighter day.”
- “A steady week.”
- “Progress is holding.”
- “Bram noticed your pull days are getting stronger.”
- “You’re building a rhythm.”

## Avoid
- “Crush your workout”
- “Unleash beast mode”
- “AI-powered hypertrophy optimization”
- “Your gains are loading”
- “Dominate the gym”

## Caslon copy moments
Use Adobe Caslon Pro for short phrases like:
- “Keep the rhythm.”
- “A steady week.”
- “A little more, at the right time.”
- “Strength, written down.”
- “Small notes. Real progress.”

### Microcopy rule
Bram should not talk too much. The product is notes-first. Bram observes, summarizes, and suggests.

- keep badge names short
- use violet, orange, and gold carefully

- avoid giant lock icons where possible

- faded sketch silhouettes
- closed notebook pages
- dimmed bear paw stamp
- subtle paper label
- soft outline illustration

- earned
- warm
- quiet pride

## 21) Light and dark mode behavior

## Dark mode
Default emotional mode:
- premium
- calm
- evening notebook
- gym-after-hours feel

Dark mode should use charcoal surfaces and violet as the signature accent.

## Light mode
Light mode should feel:
- warm paper
- clean notebook
- morning routine
- soft but serious

Light mode should not become plain white Apple clone UI. Use cream, off-white, and subtle paper warmth.

## Mode consistency rule
Bram should remain visually consistent across both modes. The world changes around Bram; Bram does not need to be redrawn entirely.

- quiet gym stills

## Texture

- generic stock athletes

## 27) Current cover direction notes

The current Bram cover direction is strong.

## What is working
- The name feels more grounded and masculine than Orson.

- The mascot now feels like a strength companion instead of a wellness companion.

## Refinements to preserve
- Keep Bram in black-bear territory, not warm brown bear territory.
- Preserve eye readability with tiny highlights and clean facial contrast.
- Keep the notebook large enough to avoid awkward open-torso space.
- Keep the notebook/barbell mark simple.
- Keep surrounding UI restrained so violet stays distinctive.
- Keep mascot cleanup surgical; avoid accidental redesigns.
