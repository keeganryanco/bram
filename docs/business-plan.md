# Bram Business Plan v1.0

**Date:** May 4, 2026
**Founder model:** Indie hacking / sweat-equity bootstrap
**App Store working name:** **Bram: Workout Tracker**
**Brand category language:** **Workout Notes**

## 1. Executive Summary

**Bram is a notes-first strength tracker for lifters who already track loosely but hate the friction of real tracking.**

The product thesis is simple:

> **Write your workout naturally. Bram tracks the rest.**

Bram sits between three broken workflows:

| Current workflow                   | Problem                                     | Bram’s answer                                |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Apple Notes / pen & paper          | Easy to write, hard to analyze              | Notes-first input + structured progress      |
| Spreadsheet                        | Powerful, annoying to maintain              | Automatic structure without spreadsheet work |
| Strong / Hevy / Liftosaur / Fitbod | Useful but setup-heavy, app-y, or overbuilt | Premium-simple daily workout notebook        |

The brand kit already defines the center: Bram is a notes-first strength-training app built around a calm black bear companion, with natural writing, quiet interpretation, progressive overload tracking, and subtle suggestions as the core experience. 

**Business objective:** launch a refined v1 quickly, validate with real users, and drive first revenue through organic distribution before reinvesting into App Store Search Ads, TikTok ads, and UGC.

**June revenue target:**
✅ **$1,000 net cash in June** can be reached with roughly **30 founding annual subscriptions at $39.99/year** after Apple’s 15% Small Business Program commission. Apple states the Small Business Program offers a reduced 15% commission for eligible developers. ([Apple Developer][1])

**MRR target math:**
A true **$1,000 net MRR** target requires about **230–280 paying subscribers**, depending on monthly/annual mix. That is aggressive, but the plan is designed around the path where it works.

## Strategic posture

Bram does not need to become the biggest fitness platform. It needs to become the **best app for people who want Apple Notes simplicity with real strength-tracking memory.**

That means the company should be built around:

* **Craft:** every screen should feel intentional.
* **Speed:** ship fast, test fast, iterate fast.
* **Clarity:** no bloated features that dilute the note-first identity.
* **Revenue-first discipline:** validate willingness to pay early.
* **Privacy-forward intelligence:** AI should feel useful, not invasive.

# 5. Ideal Customer Profile

## Primary ICP

**The self-directed modern lifter.**

They lift 3–6x/week. They have some plan, but they are not rigidly following a spreadsheet. They care about progression, hypertrophy, strength, consistency, or body composition. They understand enough to care about volume, reps, load, soreness, fatigue, and exercise selection — but they do not want to become a spreadsheet operator.

## Behavioral segments

| Segment              | Current behavior                         | Bram message                                           |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Notes lifter         | Tracks workouts in Apple Notes           | “Turn messy gym notes into progress.”                  |
| Pen-and-paper lifter | Likes tactile/simple logging             | “A notebook that remembers.”                           |
| App dropout          | Tried Strong/Hevy/Fitbod and quit        | “No setup. Just write.”                                |
| Science-aware lifter | Watches Dr. Mike, Jeff Nippard, etc.     | “Science-backed suggestions without spreadsheet work.” |
| Intuitive lifter     | Trains by feel, like Sam Sulek archetype | “Keep your style. Add memory.”                         |
| Returning lifter     | Has inconsistent history                 | “Start with today’s note.”                             |
| Busy lifter          | Wants fast logging                       | “Track in seconds, review when you want.”              |

## The most valuable early user

The best first customer is not a total beginner. It is someone who already feels the pain.

**Early buyer profile:**

* 18–35
* iPhone user
* Lifts at least 3x/week
* Has tried logging before
* Uses Notes, screenshots, spreadsheets, or abandoned fitness apps
* Likes clean design
* Has watched fitness content online
* Will pay for convenience if the product feels premium

# 6. Product Plan

## Core product promise

> **Bram turns natural workout notes into structured progress.**

## Core loop

1. User opens app.
2. Weekly date strip is at the top.
3. Today’s note is open immediately.
4. User writes naturally:

   > “Push day. Bench 185 for 3x8, last set hard. Incline DB 70s for 8/8/7. Shoulders felt cooked.”
5. Bram quietly interprets:

   * exercises
   * sets
   * reps
   * load
   * perceived effort
   * muscle groups
   * pattern/split
6. Bram shows a minimal structured summary.
7. Bram updates progress and weekly insight.
8. Bram gives one subtle suggestion:

   > “A little more pressing volume than last week. Keep shoulders lighter next push day.”

## Launch scope

The v1 should launch refined and functional with these surfaces:

| Surface            | Purpose                                                              |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Onboarding         | Goals, experience, schedule, preferred units, equipment, split style |
| Daily home         | Weekly calendar + note editor                                        |
| Note parser        | Natural note → structured workout                                    |
| Structured summary | Exercises, sets, reps, weights, notes                                |
| Progress overlay   | PRs, volume, consistency, recent exercise history                    |
| Weekly review      | Plain-language summary + one chart + one suggestion                  |
| Suggestions        | Subtle, contextual, editable/ignorable                               |
| Paywall            | RevenueCat trial/subscription                                        |
| Account            | Supabase auth, Apple/Google sign-in                                  |
| Settings           | goals, units, privacy, subscription, export/delete                   |
| Landing site       | Vercel marketing page + privacy + terms + contact                    |
| Analytics          | PostHog onboarding/product events + RevenueCat paywall metrics       |

The brand kit’s product hierarchy supports this exact shape: date/today control, open note area, interpreted workout summary, Bram suggestion, and small progress/streak indicator; weekly review should include a plain-language summary, one chart, one insight, and one suggested adjustment. 

## Not part of launch

These stay out of v1:

* social feed
* public profiles
* comments/likes
* giant exercise video library
* full routine marketplace
* complex periodization builder
* macro/calorie tracking
* wearable-first interface
* AI chat coach
* aggressive notification system

That is not “less ambition.” That is the product’s taste.

# 7. Training Philosophy

## Baseline philosophy

Bram should use a simple adaptive progressive-overload model.

Not rigid bodybuilding periodization. Not random intuitive training. A baseline that works for most self-directed lifters:

| Principle            | Bram interpretation                                                      |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Progressive overload | Track reps/load/sets over time and suggest small next steps              |
| Volume awareness     | Watch weekly set volume by muscle group                                  |
| Recovery awareness   | Notice soreness, fatigue, poor performance, missed sessions              |
| Exercise consistency | Reward stable comparisons before suggesting variation                    |
| Double progression   | Add reps before load when appropriate                                    |
| Effort awareness     | Interpret “easy,” “hard,” “grinder,” RPE/RIR language                    |
| Flexible splits      | Infer push/pull/legs, upper/lower, bro split, full body, custom patterns |
| Minimal intervention | Suggest one useful adjustment, not a full lecture                        |

## Suggestion framework

Bram suggestions should come in four levels:

| Level       | Example                                                                 |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reminder    | “You usually pull today.”                                               |
| Progression | “Last bench session was 185x8. Try matching that before adding weight.” |
| Balance     | “Back volume is ahead of chest this week.”                              |
| Recovery    | “Your leg notes mention fatigue twice. Keep today lighter.”             |

## The key

Bram should not “program” the user unless the user asks. It should **translate, remember, and refine**.

That lets both archetypes work:

* The intuitive lifter keeps writing naturally.
* The structured lifter gets clean history and progression.
* Both get value without spreadsheet work.

# 8. AI Architecture

## AI role

AI is the invisible interpretation layer.

It should not be the product identity. It should power the product identity.

## Model usage strategy

OpenAI’s current API pricing lists GPT-5.4 at **$2.50 / 1M input tokens and $15.00 / 1M output tokens**, GPT-5.5 at **$5.00 / 1M input and $30.00 / 1M output**, and GPT-5.4 mini at **$0.75 / 1M input and $4.50 / 1M output**. ([OpenAI][5])

| Task                        | Model tier                                 | Trigger                      | User experience              |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Note parsing                | cheaper fast model                         | after note save / background | instant or near-instant      |
| Exercise normalization      | cheaper fast model + deterministic mapping | after parse                  | invisible                    |
| Inline suggestion           | cheaper fast model                         | after structured summary     | one sentence                 |
| Weekly review               | GPT-5.4                                    | weekly / dashboard open      | higher quality synthesis     |
| Onboarding training profile | GPT-5.4                                    | after onboarding             | creates initial user profile |
| Complex “what should I do?” | GPT-5.4 or GPT-5.5                         | explicit user request        | premium/intelligent          |

## Monthly AI cost model

Assumptions:

* 16 workouts/month
* 1 note parse per workout
* 4 weekly reviews/month
* 1 onboarding profile generation
* Occasional retries and cached context
* Most work done by cheaper model
* Higher model used for weekly synthesis

| AI operation              |                    Estimated tokens |        Cost estimate |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------: | -------------------: |
| Note parse, 16x/mo        |    ~1,000 in / 300 out each on mini | ~$0.03–$0.05/user/mo |
| Inline suggestion, 16x/mo |      ~800 in / 150 out each on mini | ~$0.02–$0.04/user/mo |
| Weekly review, 4x/mo      | ~4,000 in / 600 out each on GPT-5.4 | ~$0.07–$0.10/user/mo |
| Onboarding amortized      |            ~3,000 in / 700 out once | ~$0.01–$0.03/user/mo |
| Buffer/retries            |                             50–100% | ~$0.10–$0.20/user/mo |

**Target AI COGS:**
✅ **$0.20–$0.40 per active paid user per month**

That makes the economics work.

## AI product rule

Every AI output must fit one of these forms:

* structured workout summary
* one-line suggestion
* weekly plain-language insight
* progression recommendation
* recovery adjustment
* exercise-history explanation

No long paragraphs. No generic coaching essays. No motivational slop.

# 9. Security & Privacy Plan

## Privacy positioning

Public promise:

> **Your workout notes are used to power your own progress. Bram does not sell your data, does not use your notes for advertising, and lets you delete your account and training history.**

OpenAI states that API data is not used to train or improve models by default unless explicitly opted in, and abuse-monitoring logs may be retained up to 30 days by default. ([OpenAI Platform][6])

## Architecture

| Layer          | Plan                                                |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Auth           | Supabase auth with Apple Sign-In + Google Sign-In   |
| User identity  | Email/name stored separately from workout data      |
| Workout data   | Linked by internal UUID                             |
| Analytics      | No note body in PostHog events                      |
| AI calls       | Send pseudonymous user/session IDs, not name/email  |
| Database       | Supabase RLS on every user-owned table              |
| Export/delete  | User can export and delete account/data             |
| HealthKit      | Optional later; not core to v1 monetization         |
| Privacy policy | Vercel-hosted, linked in App Store and app settings |

Apple’s privacy guidelines require a privacy policy identifying collected data, usage, third-party sharing, retention/deletion policies, and consent withdrawal; Apple also says personal data shared with third-party AI must be clearly disclosed and explicitly permitted. ([Apple Developer][7])

Health/fitness data has stricter treatment: Apple states HealthKit and fitness data may not be used for advertising, marketing, or use-based data mining and requires disclosure of specific health data collected. ([Apple Developer][7])

## App Store privacy stance

Bram should disclose:

* account info
* purchases
* product interaction analytics
* user-generated workout notes
* fitness data if/when HealthKit is added
* third-party AI processing for note interpretation

The tone should be confident, not defensive:

> “Bram uses your workout notes to structure your training history, generate progress insights, and provide suggestions. We do not sell workout data or use it for ads.”

# 16. Metrics Framework

## North Star

**Weekly active workout note writers.**

This captures the real habit: users writing workouts into Bram.

## Activation

A user is activated when they:

1. completes onboarding
2. writes first workout note
3. sees structured interpretation
4. returns to write or review another note within 7 days

## Key metrics

| Metric                            |      Target |
| --------------------------------- | ----------: |
| App Store product page → download |      25–40% |
| Download → onboarding started     |        70%+ |
| Onboarding started → completed    |        60%+ |
| Completed onboarding → first note |        70%+ |
| First note → parsed successfully  |        85%+ |

| Trial start from download         |       8–15% |

# 20. Final Business Thesis

Bram is a strong indie app opportunity because it is not trying to win by feature count. It is trying to win by taste.

The product is:

> **A premium workout notebook that remembers, structures, and gently improves your training.**

The market already proves people track workouts. The gap is that tracking either feels too manual, too app-like, too social, too complex, or too expensive. Bram’s wedge is the human middle: **write naturally, get progress.**

The first business milestone is not “fitness empire.” It is:

✅ ship a polished v1
✅ recruit real lifters
✅ get first paid users
✅ reach $1k cash/month or $1k MRR path
✅ reinvest into the acquisition channel that shows conversion

The whole company can be built around one sentence:

> **Progress without spreadsheets. Strength tracking without friction.**
